1 · Medicine availability

AVAILABILITY

Key points

  • 93.6% of the medicines facilities manage were on the shelf on the visit day; 87.7% of the medicines expected at their level.
  • Health posts hold 71.0% of expected medicines but 92.6% of what they manage: the gap is authorised Essential Medicines they do not stock, not stock-outs.
  • Lowest programmes: ARV 90.6% and FP 91.8%; highest Malaria 99.5%.
  • Provinces sit in a narrow band, 92.3% (West) to 94.9% (South).

2 · Stock adequacy and inventory accuracy

STOCK ADEQUACY · RECORDS

Key points

  • Only 24.0% of medicines were adequately stocked; 46.7% were overstocked (over 2 months) and 22.3% understocked.
  • 928 understocked medicines (49%) were at or below the 0.5-month emergency order point.
  • Only 56.1% of stock records matched the shelf exactly; average quantity accuracy was 69.8%.
  • Health posts keep the least accurate records (38.1% exact, 48.4% quantity); district hospitals the most (85.1% exact).

Stock adequacy by level of care

Months of stock = quantity on hand ÷ average monthly consumption (Jun–Aug); adequate 1–2 months (1–3 at CHUK and CHUB). Base 8,482 medicines, stock-outs included.

Inventory accuracy by level of care

Quantity accuracy = 1 − |system − physical| ÷ system per record, 0% at worst and where the system shows 0. 8,856 records naming a digital system.

3 · Stock-outs

STOCK-OUT · SUPPLY CHAIN

Key points

  • 9.8% of managed medicines ran out at least once in June–August; 599 were out on the visit day.
  • Stock-outs are long: 47 days on average per affected medicine; 263 of those out on the visit day had been out over 30 days.
  • 86% were distribution gaps - the medicine was at RMS Central or Regional (392 of 456); only 64 were nationwide.

4 · Prescription fulfilment

PRESCRIPTIONS

Key points

  • 99.2% of 14,059 prescribed items were available; 72 of 6,006 prescriptions (1.2%) were not fully served.
  • Lowest at national referral hospitals (84.4%); every other level is above 99%.
  • The 107 unserved items came from only 22 facilities; CHUK alone accounts for 38 (36%).
  • Prescribers tend to write what the pharmacy holds, so this rate overstates supply and is not a stock measure.

5 · Order fill rate

REQUISITIONS

Key points

  • 93.1% of units requested were delivered in June–August (35.4M of 38.1M units).
  • Vertical programmes are filled least: ARV 66.2%, FP 76.6%.
  • By level, national referral hospitals are lowest at 77.6%.
  • Monthly fill moved between 92.0% and 94.7%.

Fill rate by programme

Units delivered ÷ units requested; can exceed 100%.

6 · Community-level medicine availability

COMMUNITY

Key points

  • Community health workers had 80.1% of the medicines they manage (175 CHWs).
  • Only 69 of 175 CHWs (39.4%) held every medicine they manage.
  • Lowest in North (65.9%), highest in West (92.4%).
  • On the products both carry, CHWs had 86.2% against 99.7% at facilities.

7 · District by district

HETEROGENEITY

Key points

  • Availability ranges from 87.0% in Gicumbi to 97.5% in Burera.
  • Quantity matching varies most: 17.5% in Musanze against 100.0% in Rutsiro.
  • Most often ever had a stock-out: Ruhango (22.4%); lowest fill rate: Rubavu (84.0%).
  • 6 districts are worse than national on at least three of the four indicators: Huye, Nyagatare, Gatsibo, Rubavu, Gakenke, Gicumbi.
