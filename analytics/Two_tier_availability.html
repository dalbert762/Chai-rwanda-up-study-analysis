Tracer medicine availability: performance index and clinical diagnostics

Tier 1 measures how much of each facility's own expected basket was on the shelf. Tier 2 asks which medicines and which therapeutic categories are short — including where the Tier 1 score looks healthy. The two are reported together because a high basket score can conceal the absence of a medicine that decides whether a patient survives the day.

MethodClassifications used, and the rules applied

ItemDetail
Eligible denominatorexpected at that level per the EML, managed by the facility, availability answered. Medicines not expected at a level are excluded; unassessed medicines are excluded, never counted as unavailable.
Tier 1 / Tier 2 consistencyone absence reduces that facility's Tier 1 score and appears in that medicine's Tier 2 rate - both tiers read the same rows.
Critical listpredefined by clinical function, declared before any result was computed, listed on the 'Critical list' tab.
Therapeutic categoriesmaternal/newborn and reproductive health, antimalarials and HIV/TB from the programme field; the rest from drug class. MCCH drug-class labels are unreliable (caffeine citrate recorded as a haemostatic, tranexamic acid as an anti-apnoeic) so programme is used for them.
What a Tier 1 score is NOTit is not the probability a patient receives a prescribed medicine. Patient access is the prescription fulfilment analysis, which is a different denominator and a different tool.
